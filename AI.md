Squarkdown is actually one of the tools I’m most proud of. Even though at times it feels like it doesn’t really *do much*, and even though it’s really hard to justify to people why it exists.

But it fulfils a very particular purpose for me that has worked spectacularly across multiple projects, and that gives me all the self-satisfaction in the world. I get to build and use my own tool, couldn’t be happier. And it means I get to feel the benefits of every feature and bugfix.

This project was handcrafted with love. From the initial Ruby version (pre-agents) to the Rust rewrite (post-agents). All of the code, tests, docs and schemas are handwritten.

What I did use LLMs for:

- Discussing engineering decisions
- Helping with debugging
- Code review and writing more idiomatic Rust

I’m okay with these, since they help me learn without atrophying my mind and still leaving the joyous actual coding to me. I’ve realised I LOVE using LLMs for code review: it’s like having a senior engineer who can point out all of my sloppy work and push me to do better.
